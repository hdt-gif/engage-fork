Planning question

What is the cheapest way to meet the island's electricity needs?

Engage finds the cheapest plan that balances energy every hour. An island grid also has to hold spinning reserve for a sudden loss of a unit or a cloud over the solar, and oil units that are on can't run below their minimum. This add-on brings four GridPath features to that question: operating reserves, capacity credit, planning several periods at once, and deciding which old plants to retire.

GridPath feature · resource adequacy with ELCC

Does the plan have enough capacity for the tightest hour?

Engage's reserve margin counts every megawatt the same, so 1 MW of solar counts as 1 MW even at 7 pm when the sun is down. GridPath gives each resource a capacity credit (its effective load-carrying capability, or ELCC): how much it actually reduces the firm capacity the island needs in its tightest hour. Credits shrink as more of the same resource is added, and solar and batteries are worth more together than apart.

What each resource counts for

Each extra 100 MW of battery: how much counts as firm?

Battery credit falls as more is added, because the tightest hours get longer and there's less spare energy to recharge. More solar keeps battery credit higher for longer, because it supplies the charging energy.

GridPath feature · multiple investment periods

What should be built, and when, to reach 100% by 2045?

Engage optimizes one year per run. To plan a path you'd run 2030, carry its capacity into a 2040 run by hand, then do the same for 2045. GridPath plans every period in one run. What it builds in 2030 is chosen knowing what 2045 will need, and each period's capacity carries forward automatically.

GridPath feature · economic retirement

Which oil units are worth keeping?

In Engage, existing plants are a fixed input: you decide what stays. GridPath can make retirement a decision. For each unit it weighs the cost of keeping it running (staff, maintenance, fuel at its efficiency) against what it would cost to replace what it does. How this attaches to Engage
1 · Read the Engage model

A new Django app, reliability, reads the run's locations, technologies, timeseries and scenario parameters from Engage's Postgres database. Nothing in Engage's own tables changes.

2 · Solve with GridPath

It writes GridPath's input CSVs, adds the settings from these tabs (reserves, reserve margin and capacity credits, periods, unit-level plant data), and solves on the Celery worker Engage already uses for Calliope, with the open-source HiGHS solver.

3 · Show it on the run

The results come back as this tab on the run, side by side with Engage's own result.

In this prototype, simplified calculations in your browser stand in for GridPath: a grid search over build sizes, five representative days, a peak-hour capacity-credit method, a step-by-step search for the pathway, and a one-unit-at-a-time retirement search. The real add-on solves each as one GridPath optimization over every hour of the year.
